Cyberduck is the best-known free file-transfer client. Its sibling, Mountain Duck, mounts remote storage as a drive. Buckets is a paid desktop app built only for Amazon S3, CloudFront, and S3-compatible storage.

The two products start from different places. Cyberduck is a general file-transfer tool that supports S3 among many protocols. Buckets is an S3 and CloudFront console.

## Choose Cyberduck or Mountain Duck if…

- **You need more than S3.** Cyberduck browses and transfers over many protocols in one app. Buckets is built around S3: it has no FTP support, and it can only mount SFTP, WebDAV and FTPS servers as a folder through rclone, not browse them in the app.
- **You want a native drive without extra installs.** Mountain Duck uses the operating system's own cloud-file APIs. Buckets' Virtual Drive works, but it relies on rclone and a FUSE driver that you install separately.
- **You want free.** Cyberduck costs nothing. Buckets has a 14-day full trial and no free tier.
- **You prefer open source.**

## Choose Buckets if…

- **Sign-in is your daily pain.** Problems with IAM Identity Center and AssumeRole sessions come up repeatedly in Cyberduck's public issue tracker (for example [#13377](https://github.com/iterate-ch/cyberduck/issues/13377) and [#17694](https://github.com/iterate-ch/cyberduck/issues/17694)). Buckets runs its own SSO device flow with automatic refresh. It also runs `aws login` in an isolated sandbox and switches between saved accounts in one click. Try it with your own organization during the trial.
- **You administer S3, not just move files.** Buckets covers bucket policies, lifecycle rules, Object Lock, replication, Batch Operations, Storage Lens, a security and PII scanner, and IaC export to CDK, CloudFormation, SAM, or Terraform.
- **You run sites on CloudFront.** Buckets supports Origin Access Control and targeted invalidations, with a cost preview: the first 1,000 paths each month are free. It can also sign URLs and cookies, test and publish CloudFront Functions, and deploy a local folder to a private bucket behind CloudFront.
- **You want to see costs before you incur them.** Billable operations show an estimate and the permissions they need before they run.
- **You need point-in-time recovery.** Time Travel rebuilds a prefix's earlier state from retained versions and delete markers, and you review the plan before you apply it. It works from the version history S3 kept: a plan covers up to 200,000 versions and delete markers, incomplete plans are refused, and permanently deleted versions cannot be brought back.
